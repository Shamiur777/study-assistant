# The Kidneys

## Structure
The kidneys are two bean-shaped organs at the back of the abdomen. Each kidney has an outer cortex, an inner medulla and a renal pelvis that collects urine and drains into the ureter. The functional unit of the kidney is the nephron. A nephron includes the glomerulus, Bowman's capsule, the proximal convoluted tubule, the loop of Henle, the distal convoluted tubule and the collecting duct.

## Urine formation
Urine forms in three steps. First, filtration happens in the glomerulus, where blood pressure pushes water and small solutes into Bowman's capsule. Second, reabsorption returns useful substances such as glucose, amino acids and most water to the blood. Third, secretion moves extra waste and ions from the blood into the tubule.

## Functions
The kidneys remove waste products such as urea and keep the body's water and salt levels balanced. They help control blood pressure by releasing the enzyme renin. They produce the hormone erythropoietin, which stimulates red blood cell production, and they activate vitamin D.
