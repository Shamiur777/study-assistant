# The Digestive System

## Pathway
Food enters the mouth and is swallowed into the esophagus, which carries it to the stomach by peristalsis. From the stomach it moves into the small intestine, which has three parts: the duodenum, jejunum and ileum. Undigested material then passes to the large intestine and is stored in the rectum before elimination.

## Digestive enzymes
Salivary amylase in the mouth begins breaking down starch. In the stomach, pepsin digests protein in strongly acidic conditions created by hydrochloric acid. The pancreas releases lipase, which digests fats, and trypsin, which digests proteins, into the small intestine. The liver makes bile, which is stored in the gallbladder and emulsifies fats so lipase can work on them.

## Absorption
Most nutrient absorption happens in the small intestine, whose inner wall is covered with finger-like villi and microscopic microvilli that greatly increase surface area. The large intestine mainly absorbs water and salts and hosts helpful gut bacteria.
